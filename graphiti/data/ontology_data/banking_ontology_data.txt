INDIAN BANKING SECTOR — RAW FACT COMPILATION
Listed Companies in India (all NSE/BSE tickers), covering the entity and relationship content of the accompanying ontology, written as plain sourced prose rather than as structured entity/relationship notation.

Scope: 39 listed Indian banks — 12 Public Sector Banks, 20 Private Sector Banks, 7 Small Finance Banks — as of September 15, 2026. No Payments Bank or Foreign Bank currently trades as a standalone listing on an Indian exchange, so those two segments of the ontology have no member companies here. (One exception worth flagging: Fino Payments Bank Limited, ticker FINOPB, is listed and is licensed as a Payments Bank, but it was not researched in this pass, which was scoped to PSU, Private and Small Finance Banks only.)

Methodology note: most figures below are for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 (Q1 FY27), the most recent common reporting period available at the time this was compiled. Figures that could not be verified from a reliable source are said to be not found rather than estimated. A few figures that appeared internally inconsistent across sources are called out explicitly as unresolved rather than silently picked. Regulators common to every bank in this document are the Reserve Bank of India (licensing, Basel III capital norms, priority-sector-lending mandates) and the Securities and Exchange Board of India (listed-company disclosure, minimum public shareholding, takeover code). Credit rating agencies referenced throughout are CRISIL Ratings, ICRA, CARE Ratings, India Ratings (part of the Fitch group), and, occasionally, Fitch Ratings directly. No investment recommendations (buy, sell, hold, or a target price) appear anywhere in this document.

STATE BANK OF INDIA (ticker SBIN, NSE and BSE)

State Bank of India is India's largest bank by assets, headquartered in Mumbai, Maharashtra. It is a universal bank run on a hybrid model that combines its huge branch network with a digital-first push through its YONO platform. The Government of India is SBI's majority owner, holding 55.03% as of 31 March 2026; LIC holds 8.70%, mutual funds and UTI hold 13.57%, and FIIs/NRIs/GDRs hold 12.49% (all as of the same date, source: hellobanker.in's published shareholding pattern for March 2026). The bank's top executive, under the SBI Act, carries the title Chairman rather than MD & CEO; C S Setty has held that role since his appointment was reported in August 2024 (source: newsonair.gov.in).

SBI offers the full range of retail and corporate banking products — savings, current and fixed deposits, home, auto, personal, education, agricultural and MSME loans, credit and debit cards — and provides wealth management, trade finance, treasury and forex services, cash management, investment banking through its subsidiary SBI Caps, and NRI banking. It serves retail mass-market customers, HNIs, MSMEs, large corporates, government and institutional clients, and the NRI community. It runs roughly 22,500 branches and 65,000 ATMs (a figure cited in a government public statement via Business Today, not independently re-verified this session), making it easily the largest branch network in the country, and it operates internationally as well, though a precise current count of overseas branches was not found.

On funding, SBI's CASA ratio stood at 39.24% in the June 2026 quarter (source: Investing.com's summary of SBI's Q1 FY27 investor slides, August 2026), a low-cost and stable base; total deposits were ₹60.06 trillion, up 9.73% year-on-year. Its domestic loan book (advances) reached ₹42.76 trillion, up 18.15% year-on-year, and net interest income was ₹46,992 crore, up 14.88%. The bank uses Finacle-based core banking technology and has invested in AI-driven credit underwriting.

Analytically, SBI's principal competitive advantage is treated here as its unmatched branch, ATM and digital scale combined with sovereign backing — a claim rated high confidence and directly evidenced by the ownership and network figures above. On the strength side, the bank posted record profitability with the lowest non-performing-asset levels in more than two decades (high confidence, direct evidence from the Q1 FY27 slides). A fair weakness is that its domestic net interest margin, at 3.00%, trails several private-bank peers (medium confidence, direct evidence). An opportunity worth noting, with more moderate confidence since it rests on inference rather than a specific disclosed initiative, is that the YONO platform's scale could be used for further cross-selling. A threat, also inferred rather than directly stated by the bank, is those same private banks — HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank chief among them — continuing to take retail and digital market share.

SBI is exposed to credit risk through its large corporate loan book, interest-rate risk given the size of its deposit base, cyber risk given the scale of its digital user base, and ordinary regulatory risk. It depends on continued regulatory support from the RBI and Ministry of Finance, on the uptime of its YONO platform, and on its branch network for distribution.

Its other Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 2026) metrics were: return on assets 1.11%, return on equity 17.87%, cost-to-income ratio 46.71%, gross NPA 1.47%, net NPA 0.38%, provision coverage ratio 74.20% (91.82% including technical write-offs), and capital adequacy ratio 15.67% (a precise CET1 percentage was not found, though the bank states it is comfortably above the regulatory minimum of 8.80%). On credit ratings, SBI has historically sat in the Moody's Baa2/Baa3 and S&P/Fitch BBB- range on long-term issuer and deposit ratings (per older Business Standard archive coverage), but no dated 2026 rating action could be confirmed this session.

Looking at what drives the bank going forward: growth is expected to come from retail credit demand and digital acquisition via YONO; margin from the CASA mix and the trajectory of the repo rate; return on equity from the operating leverage that comes with scale; the main risk driver is asset quality in unsecured retail and MSME lending; and the qualitative valuation question is how the market prices a sovereign-owned bank against private peers, with no target price implied. SBI is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license. As with every bank in this document, the RBI repo rate influences its net interest margin through deposit and loan repricing, and India's GDP growth influences loan demand.

BANK OF BARODA (ticker BANKBARODA, NSE and BSE)

Bank of Baroda, headquartered in Vadodara, Gujarat, is a universal bank and a Government of India-owned public sector bank with the widest overseas footprint of any PSU bank besides SBI, present in 24 countries. Debadatta Chand is MD & CEO (source: Wikipedia and the bank's card-subsidiary site, bobcard.co.in); the current non-executive chairman could not be confirmed this session, and the exact current government shareholding percentage was likewise not found.

The bank offers the standard deposit and loan product suite, issues credit cards through its BOBCARD subsidiary, and provides trade finance, forex, treasury, cash management, wealth management and NRI banking services, serving retail, MSME, large-corporate, NRI and government customers. It runs more than 8,400 branches and over 10,000 ATMs as of September 2025 (Wikipedia), plus 107 international branches and offices across those 24 countries and a joint-venture bank in Zambia with 16 branches.

Its Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was 37.72%, down from 38.88% a year earlier, as term deposits (up 17.8%) and bulk deposits/certificates of deposit (up 37.0%) outgrew low-cost CASA balances — a sign of a mild shift toward costlier wholesale funding (source: Investing.com's summary of the bank's Q1 FY27 slides, August 2026). Cost of deposits fell 39 basis points year-on-year to 4.66%. Global advances grew 17.4% year-on-year; global deposits reached ₹16,33,559 crore, up 13.8%; domestic deposits were ₹13,81,535 crore, up 14.7%. Net interest income was ₹12,524 crore, up 9.5%.

Analytically, the bank's 24-country network is treated as a real but only medium-confidence competitive advantage — the branch count is a direct fact, but its strategic value is inferential. The clearest weakness this quarter was financial: reported net profit fell 72% year-on-year to ₹1,278 crore because of a ₹5,680 crore legal settlement tied to the long-running NMC Health matter (adjusted profit, stripping that out, was ₹5,528 crore) — a high-confidence, directly evidenced fact from Business Standard and PSUConnect coverage in July 2026. On the opportunity side, ICRA assigned an AAA rating to a ₹10,000 crore green infrastructure bond programme in February 2026, a medium-confidence signal of growing green-finance capacity. The main threat flagged is the funding-cost pressure implied by rising reliance on bulk and CD funding as CASA declines.

The bank is exposed to regulatory and litigation risk (illustrated by the NMC settlement), ordinary credit risk, funding risk from CASA erosion, and the operational complexity of running banking operations across 24 jurisdictions. It depends on the RBI and multiple overseas regulators, on wholesale and bulk deposit funding, and on Gulf and African NRI remittance corridors geographically.

Other Q1 FY27 metrics (source: PSUConnect and Investing.com, July-August 2026): return on assets 0.25% as reported, or 1.10% excluding the one-off; return on equity 3.89% reported, or 16.57% excluding the one-off; net interest margin 2.77% globally and 2.93% domestically; gross NPA 1.99% (down 29 basis points year-on-year); net NPA 0.50%; provision coverage 93.28% including technical write-offs or 75.07% excluding them; capital adequacy 16.30% with CET1 of 13.90% on a standalone basis (consolidated capital adequacy 16.70%, CET1 14.36%). Its AT-1 bonds were reaffirmed ICRA AA+ Stable, with CET1 separately noted at 12.45% in that February 2026 rating rationale — a figure that does not neatly reconcile with the headline capital numbers above and is left unresolved here. Growth is expected to be driven by the global loan book and the international franchise; margin by deposit-cost management; return on equity by normalization once the one-off NMC drag passes; the key risk driver is legal and contingent-liability exposure; and the valuation-relevant fact, stated without any recommendation, is simply that reported and adjusted profitability diverge sharply this quarter because of the one-off item. Bank of Baroda is regulated by the RBI, SEBI and various overseas banking regulators, and holds a universal banking license.

PUNJAB NATIONAL BANK (ticker PNB, NSE and BSE)

Punjab National Bank, headquartered in Dwarka, New Delhi, is a Government of India-owned universal bank. Non-executive chairman K G Ananthakrishnan and MD & CEO Ashok Chandra lead the bank (source: Wikipedia). Government ownership was 70.08% as of 31 March 2026 (source: hellobanker.in's shareholding-pattern release for FY26, June 2026); FIIs/NRIs/OCBs held 6.59%, mutual funds 6.53%, and banks/financial institutions/insurers together 9.42% (LIC's specific share within that last figure was not found).

PNB offers the standard deposit and loan suite plus gold coins and bullion, and distributes life and non-life insurance and asset-management products alongside trade finance, treasury, forex and cash management services; its customer base skews toward MSME and agriculture in the north Indian market it has historically dominated. It runs 10,324 branches and 11,065 ATMs as of March 2026 (Wikipedia), and has an international presence through seven UK branches plus branches in Hong Kong, Dubai and Afghanistan, representative offices in Kazakhstan, the UAE, China, Norway and Australia, and equity stakes in banks in Bhutan (51%), Nepal (20%) and Kazakhstan (41.64%).

Its Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was 36.7% of domestic deposits (source: Investing.com's summary of PNB's Q1 FY27 slides), a reasonably stable low-cost funding base. Advances grew 12.7% year-on-year and deposits 8.5%. Net interest income growth was muted at just 2.1% year-on-year, reaching ₹10,798 crore.

The bank's clearest strength this quarter, and a high-confidence, directly evidenced one, is a sharp asset-quality turnaround: gross NPA more than halved from its historical highs to 2.78%, with provision coverage at 97.23%. Net profit rose 213.6% year-on-year to ₹5,253 crore. The corresponding weakness is that net interest income growth of only 2.1% is lagging the pace of balance-sheet growth. An opportunity worth noting is that CET1 capital improved to 14.52% from 10.95% a year earlier, giving the bank more room to fund growth. The lingering threat, assessed with only moderate confidence since it is an inference from history rather than a current disclosed fact, is that PNB's legacy of large corporate NPAs (including past fraud incidents) remains a structural weak point relative to cleaner peers.

PNB is exposed to credit risk from legacy corporate exposures, operational risk given that fraud history, geographic concentration risk in the north, and ordinary regulatory risk; it depends on the RBI and on the technology integration that followed its 2020 amalgamation with Oriental Bank of Commerce and United Bank of India. Other Q1 FY27 metrics (source: Investing.com, July 2026): return on assets 1.04%, return on equity 17.33% annualized, net interest margin 2.50% globally and 2.64% domestically, cost-to-income ratio 50.31%, net NPA 0.28%, capital adequacy 18.13%. Fitch upgraded PNB's Viability Rating from bb- to bb and affirmed its Long-Term Issuer Default Rating at BBB- with a Stable outlook, noting an impaired-loan ratio of 3.2% over the nine months to that point (source: PSUConnect, February 2026). Growth is expected to come from MSME and retail credit in northern markets; margin from an improving CASA mix; return on equity from the continued normalization of provisioning after the asset-quality cleanup; the key risk driver is residual legacy corporate exposure; and the valuation-relevant fact is that improved capital adequacy is now supporting the funding of further growth. PNB is regulated by the RBI and SEBI, holds a universal banking license, and maintains persistent overseas banking joint ventures in Bhutan, Nepal and Kazakhstan.

CANARA BANK (ticker CANBK, NSE and BSE)

Canara Bank, headquartered in Bengaluru, Karnataka, is a Government of India-owned universal bank with a strong South Indian retail, agricultural and MSME franchise. Brajesh Kumar Singh serves as MD & CEO according to the bank's own communications; the current non-executive chairman could not be confirmed this session because sources conflicted (Wikipedia names Vijay Srirangan, while a separate MarketScreener item states he ceased to hold that role). Government of India ownership was 62.93% as of March 2026 (source: Business Upturn).

The bank offers the standard product range and provides trade finance, treasury, forex, cash management, and insurance and asset-management distribution through its Canara HSBC Life Insurance and Canara Robeco Asset Management joint ventures, which are ongoing, persistent partnerships rather than one-off deals. It runs 9,861 branches and a combination of 7,907 ATMs and 2,940 cash-recycling machines as of June 2025 (Wikipedia), and has international operations in the UK, UAE, US, Hong Kong, China, Bahrain and Tanzania.

Canara Bank's Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was 29.70% (source: Investing.com, July 2026), lower than several peers that run CASA ratios above 35%, which makes it comparatively more sensitive to funding-cost swings. Advances grew 17.97% year-on-year and deposits 11.63%. Net interest income crossed ₹10,000 crore for the first time, reaching ₹10,215 crore.

The bank's asset quality is a clear, high-confidence strength: gross NPA of 1.57%, net NPA of 0.36%, and provision coverage of 94.76% — among the strongest profiles of any PSU peer in this document — alongside strong profitability ratios (return on equity 18.31%, return on assets 1.04%). The corresponding weakness is that net profit grew only about 2% year-on-year despite the strong net-interest-income and advances growth, implying margin compression or heavier provisioning elsewhere in the P&L. The clearest opportunity is that 17.97% advances growth is outpacing several peers, while the main threat is that the comparatively low CASA ratio raises the bank's relative sensitivity to funding costs. No current, dated credit rating for Canara Bank could be confirmed this session.

Canara Bank is exposed to funding risk given its lower CASA base, credit risk tied to the seasonality of its agricultural book, interest-rate risk and ordinary regulatory risk; it depends on the south Indian rural and monsoon-linked agricultural economy and on its branch density in Karnataka and the wider south. Other Q1 FY27 metrics (source: Investing.com, July 2026): cost-to-income ratio 49.03%, capital adequacy 17.17%, CET1 12.91%. Growth is expected to come from continued agricultural, MSME and retail credit expansion; margin from the CASA mobilization the bank needs given its below-average base; return on equity from strong provisioning discipline, evidenced by that high coverage ratio; the key risk driver is monsoon-linked agricultural seasonality; and the valuation-relevant fact is the bank's asset-quality strength relative to peers. Canara Bank is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license.

UNION BANK OF INDIA (ticker UNIONBANK, NSE and BSE)

Union Bank of India, headquartered at Union Bank Bhavan, Nariman Point, Mumbai, is a Government of India-owned universal bank undergoing a digital-transformation push branded "Vyom." Asheesh Pandey was appointed MD & CEO in 2026, succeeding A. Manimekhalai on completion of her tenure (source: HRKatha, Exchange4Media and MarketScreener coverage). The current non-executive chairman could not be confirmed — Wikipedia names Srinivasan Varadarajan, but a separate MarketScreener item suggests he ceased to hold the role, and this was not resolved. Government ownership was reported at roughly 74.76% by one source (ChoiceIndia), though the exact reporting period behind that figure is ambiguous; institutional holders were cited as FII 8.63%, mutual funds 4.73%, insurance 6.70%, other domestic institutions 0.55% and retail 4.63%, all figures of uncertain vintage and best treated as indicative rather than precise.

The bank offers the standard retail, MSME, agricultural and corporate product suite and the usual trade finance, treasury, forex and cash-management services, with an international presence in Hong Kong, Sydney, Dubai, the UK, Shanghai, Beijing and Abu Dhabi (Wikipedia). Its branch count was 8,621 with 8,910 ATMs as of March 2025; a later, unconfirmed 2026 reference put the figures closer to 8,697 branches (including two overseas) and 8,656 ATMs.

A precise current CASA ratio was not found this session, though management referenced average CASA balances exceeding ₹24,000 crore on a recent earnings call without stating a clear ratio. Advances growth of 12.50% year-on-year and deposit growth of just 3.50% were both cited only in a single lower-confidence secondary source (a social-media post summarizing the bank's investor presentation, July 2026) and should be treated cautiously and re-verified against the bank's own investor-presentation PDF; management has separately stated an ambition to grow credit by 18-20% going forward. Net interest income surpassed ₹10,000 crore, reaching a reported ₹10,037 crore, up 10.14% year-on-year, again per that same secondary source.

The bank's clearest strength this quarter is financial: standalone net profit rose 29.5% year-on-year to ₹5,332 crore (consolidated ₹5,641 crore), and gross NPA improved to 2.65% from 3.52% a year earlier, with net NPA at 0.47% (source: IndiaInfoline, July 2026). A weakness flagged with low confidence, because it rests on that single secondary source rather than a primary filing, is that deposit growth may be lagging advances growth significantly. The clearer, better-evidenced opportunity is that capital adequacy of 18.46% gives the bank headroom to pursue its stated 18-20% credit-growth target.

Union Bank is exposed to funding and liquidity risk if the flagged deposit-growth lag proves real, ordinary credit and interest-rate risk, and technology risk tied to the post-merger integration that followed its amalgamation with Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank. Other Q1 FY27 metrics: return on assets 1.36%, described by management as having held for three consecutive quarters; net interest margin 2.80% per the earnings-call transcript; capital adequacy 18.46%. A separate February 2026 Fitch-linked release cited CET1 at 15.7%, a figure that does not obviously reconcile with the capital-adequacy number above and is left unresolved. Fitch also upgraded the bank's Viability Rating from bb- to bb and affirmed its Long-Term Issuer Default Rating at BBB- Stable. Growth is expected to come from management's stated 18-20% credit-growth ambition; margin from continued cost-to-income improvement (reported as down 500 basis points year-on-year, though the resulting current level was not found); return on equity from the sustained roughly 1.36% return on assets; the key risk driver is the pace of deposit mobilization relative to that growth ambition; and the valuation-relevant fact is simply the capital headroom the bank currently holds. Union Bank of India is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license.

BANK OF INDIA (ticker BANKINDIA, NSE and BSE)

Bank of India, headquartered at the Bandra Kurla Complex in Mumbai, Maharashtra, is a Government of India-owned universal bank with a sizeable international operation relative to its balance sheet. Rajneesh Karnatak is MD & CEO, with his term extended by three years per a corporate announcement (source: ScanX). Government ownership was cited at 73.38% by Angel One, though the reporting period behind that figure was not specified.

The bank offers the standard retail, MSME, agricultural and corporate product range plus trade finance, treasury, forex and cash-management services, and has an international presence through five subsidiaries and one joint venture abroad (Wikipedia). Its domestic branch count was 5,202, with 8,166 ATMs; the overseas branch count is genuinely unclear, with one figure citing 60 overseas branches and another, from the same source, citing 22 — the two were not reconciled here.

Its Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was 36.68% (source: Investing.com's earnings-call transcript summary, July 2026), a reasonably stable base. Global advances grew 18.84% year-on-year to ₹7.98 lakh crore, with domestic gross advances up 19.20% to ₹6.74 lakh crore, while global deposits grew 14.90% to ₹9.58 lakh crore. Net interest income was ₹6,833 crore, up 12.61%. One headline separately cited a much lower "consolidated" net profit figure of ₹1,747.39 crore that does not reconcile with the ₹3,068 crore standalone figure reported elsewhere — this inconsistency is flagged rather than resolved.

The bank's strongest, most directly evidenced points this quarter were asset-quality improvement — gross NPA down 111 basis points year-on-year to 1.81%, net NPA down 24 basis points to 0.51%, provision coverage at 93.63% — and a 36.23% year-on-year rise in standalone net profit to ₹3,068 crore. A weakness worth flagging with low confidence, since it is an inference about sustainability rather than a stated fact, is that the bank's very low credit cost (0.15%) and slippage ratio (0.24%) this quarter may not hold through a full credit cycle. A further low-confidence point, based on the profit-figure discrepancy noted above, is that the bank's disclosure consistency between standalone and consolidated reporting merits some scrutiny.

Bank of India is exposed to ordinary credit and interest-rate risk, to the operational/disclosure-consistency question just raised, and to regulatory risk; it depends on deposit growth (14.90% year-on-year) keeping pace with its faster advances growth (18.84%). Other Q1 FY27 metrics (source: Investing.com, July 2026): return on assets 1.01%, net interest margin 2.52% globally, cost-to-income ratio 46.3%, capital adequacy 18.69% (up from 17.39%). No current, dated credit rating for Bank of India could be confirmed this session. Growth is expected to come from strong domestic and global advances growth; margin from NIM stability near 2.5%; return on equity from continued improvement in credit cost and slippage trends; the key risk driver is whether that unusually low credit cost proves sustainable; and the valuation-relevant fact is the bank's improved capital-adequacy position. Bank of India is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license.

INDIAN BANK (ticker INDIANB, NSE and BSE)

Indian Bank, headquartered in Chennai, Tamil Nadu, is a Government of India-owned universal bank that became the seventh-largest PSU bank after merging with Allahabad Bank on 1 April 2020 (Wikipedia). Binod Kumar has been MD & CEO since his appointment in January 2025 (source: Business Standard); the current non-executive chairman was not found. Government ownership stood at 73.84% (source: CRISIL's rating rationale for the bank, dated 6 February 2026); LIC's and other major institutional holdings were not verified with a current percentage in this pass.

Management describes a strategic focus on RAM lending — retail, agriculture and MSME — plus infrastructure lending, with a stated target of roughly 95% of transactions being digital (source: The Tribune, quoting the MD, 2026). The bank offers the standard deposit and loan suite alongside trade finance, forex, treasury, cash management and wealth-management services, serving retail, MSME, agricultural, corporate, government/institutional and NRI customers. It runs 6,001 branches and 5,657 ATMs and cash-recycling machines (Wikipedia), plus international branches in Colombo and Singapore and a correspondent-banking network of 227 banks across 75 countries.

A precise Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was not found, but deposits grew 13.5% year-on-year (source: indianmasterminds.com, July 2026) and credit grew 14%. Total income for the quarter was ₹20,723.62 crore, of which ₹18,090.16 crore, or roughly 87%, was interest income, implying relatively modest fee income by comparison.

The bank's clearest strengths, both high-confidence and directly evidenced, are its asset quality — gross NPA improved to 1.86%, net NPA to 0.15%, provision coverage 98.22% — and its capital position, with capital adequacy of 17.58% and CET1 of 16.51%, comfortably above the regulatory minimum. A weakness worth noting with low confidence is that the current non-executive chairman could not be identified in the public sources reviewed, suggesting somewhat limited recent disclosure visibility on that point. The clearer opportunity is the bank's stated strategic pivot toward RAM and infrastructure lending.

Indian Bank is exposed to credit risk from infrastructure and corporate concentration, interest-rate risk, technology risk given the scale of its digitization push, and ordinary regulatory risk; it depends on its retail deposit franchise in South India and on the Tamil Nadu regional economy. Credit ratings from CRISIL, reaffirmed 6 February 2026, place the bank at AAA Stable on its Tier II Basel III bonds and infrastructure bonds, AA+ Stable on Additional Tier I bonds, and A1+ on its certificate-of-deposit programme. Growth is expected to come from the RAM and infrastructure push and continued digital-transaction growth; margin from deposit repricing and CASA mobilization; return on equity from sustained profitability growth (net profit up roughly 10% year-on-year) and capital efficiency; the key risk driver is corporate and infrastructure loan concentration; and the valuation-relevant fact is that sovereign ownership underpins the stability of those top-tier CRISIL ratings. Indian Bank is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license.

CENTRAL BANK OF INDIA (ticker CENTRALBK, NSE and BSE)

Central Bank of India, headquartered in Mumbai, Maharashtra and founded in 1911, is a Government of India-owned universal bank with a legacy, branch-heavy network and a retail, MSME and agricultural focus. Kalyan Kumar took charge as MD & CEO on 1 October 2025 (source: Exchange4media); Tapan Ray is reported as non-executive chairman per Wikipedia, though this was not independently re-verified. Government ownership was 89.27% as of 31 March 2025, a figure still being referenced into 2026 filings (source: Trendlyne); LIC held 3.16% at the same date, mutual funds about 0.22% and FIIs about 1.3%.

The bank's Q1 FY27 CASA ratio was a notably strong 46.61% (source: Investing.com/Business Standard, July 2026), among the higher levels in the PSU peer group and a genuine funding-cost advantage. Total advances reached ₹354,348 crore, up a striking 28.58% year-on-year, though deposit growth of 11.68% lagged well behind that, which is itself a mild funding-risk signal. Net interest income rose 15.70% to ₹3,914 crore, with a net interest margin of 3.06%. The most recently verified branch and ATM counts — 4,608 branches and 3,644 ATMs — date to March 2021, and no current figure could be found.

The bank's strengths this quarter, both directly evidenced, are that high CASA ratio and the rapid advances growth just described. A clear weakness is a still-elevated cost-to-income ratio of 55.40%, and gross NPA, while improved, remains at 2.60%, a legacy overhang relative to sub-2% peers. The main opportunity is that continued deposit and loan growth is outpacing several peers; the main threat is precisely the funding-gap risk implied by advances outgrowing deposits so much this quarter.

Central Bank of India is exposed to funding risk from that advances-deposits gap, ordinary credit risk, and operational risk tied to its legacy, branch-heavy network; it depends on Government of India capital support and, historically, on the RBI's Prompt Corrective Action framework, which the bank has previously been subject to. Other Q1 FY27 metrics: return on assets 1.00%, return on equity 14.92%, net NPA 0.49%, provision coverage 95.86%, capital adequacy 18.28% with CET1 of 16.54%. India Ratings reaffirmed an IND AA Stable rating on the bank's Tier II bonds. Growth is expected to come from rapid credit expansion and CASA-led deposit mobilization; margin from that high CASA base supporting net interest margin despite modest year-on-year compression; return on equity from improving profitability; the key risk driver is the funding gap if deposit growth continues to lag credit growth; and the valuation-relevant fact is the legacy gross-NPA overhang relative to peers with sub-2% NPA levels. Central Bank of India is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a universal banking license.

INDIAN OVERSEAS BANK (ticker IOB, NSE and BSE)

Indian Overseas Bank, headquartered in Chennai, Tamil Nadu and founded in 1937, is a Government of India-owned universal bank with a meaningful legacy corporate and international book alongside its retail and MSME push. Ajay Kumar Srivastava's tenure as MD & CEO was extended until 2027 (source: the bank's own social-media announcement and indianmasterminds.com); Srinivasan Sridhar was appointed part-time non-official director and non-executive chairman (source: MarketScreener India). Government ownership was reported at 92.44% in a SEBI compliance filing of uncertain period (source: scanx.trade); the government separately launched an offer-for-sale to sell up to 3% of its stake in December 2025 (source: Business Standard), implying the post-sale holding is somewhat lower than 92.44%, though the exact resulting figure was not found.

The bank offers the standard product suite, provides trade finance, forex, treasury and cash-management services including an offshore banking unit in Colombo, and serves retail, MSME, agricultural, corporate and NRI customers, including the Tamil diaspora through its overseas offices. Its most recently verified branch and ATM counts — 3,236 branches, 3,506 ATMs, and 6,379 banking correspondents — date to March 2024. It maintains representative offices in Guangzhou, Kuala Lumpur and Dubai and a 35% stake in a Malaysian joint-venture bank, India International Bank (Malaysia).

Its Q1 FY27 global CASA ratio fell to 41.05% from 43.78% a year earlier, a mild funding-cost headwind (source: Business Standard, 21 July 2026). Global advances reached ₹3.22 trillion, up 22.75% year-on-year, and the credit-deposit ratio rose 630 basis points to 85.63%, which raises liquidity-management questions. Net interest income rose 34.30% to ₹3,688 crore, with domestic net interest margin at 3.48% and global margin at 3.37%.

The clearest strength, high-confidence and directly evidenced, is asset quality: gross NPA improved to 1.33% from 1.97% a year earlier, with net NPA at 0.18% and provision coverage at 97.67%, alongside a 49.32% rise in net profit to ₹1,659 crore. The corresponding weaknesses are a sharp rise in cost-to-income, from 44.22% to 53.95% year-on-year, and the CASA decline noted above. The main threat is that rising credit-deposit ratio increasing liquidity and funding pressure.

SURYODAY SMALL FINANCE BANK LIMITED (ticker SURYODAY, NSE; 543279, BSE)

Suryoday Small Finance Bank, headquartered in Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, was incorporated in 2008 as Suryoday Micro Finance Pvt Ltd, received RBI in-principle approval for a small-finance-bank license in September 2015, commenced banking operations on 23 January 2017, and listed on 26 March 2021. Baskar Babu Ramachandran, co-founder, has been MD & CEO for roughly 18.7 years — the longest tenure of any founder-CEO among the small finance banks in this document — and Krishna Nair is independent, non-executive part-time chairman. Rather than a single corporate promoter entity, the bank's promoters are a group of founding individuals: Baskar Babu Ramachandran himself at 5.22%, Sheela Pai Cole at 4.18%, Sunaina Pai Ocalan at 4.18%, and P. S. Jagdish at 3.32%, for a combined promoter group of 22.48% as of June 2026, stable since March 2024.

The bank runs an urban and semi-urban microfinance-origin model; a financial-data aggregator has flagged that "other income," including gains, forms a notably large part of the bank's reported profit in recent periods, which is worth bearing in mind when assessing earnings quality. Q1 FY27 deposits were ₹10,596 crore, up 16.3% year-on-year; the gross loan book was ₹9,074 crore, up 22.0% — the smallest loan book of any small finance bank in this document. As of June 2026, promoters held 22.48% of the bank, domestic institutions 5.47%, foreign institutions 5.35%, and the public a notably high 66.70% — an unusually diffuse ownership structure for this peer group. Net interest income was ₹622.36 crore on an annualized-equivalent basis, up 25.69%; the same aggregator separately flags roughly ₹399 crore of "other income" as a large contributor to recent profit.

Net profit rising 113.10% year-on-year to ₹75.18 crore, and capital adequacy of 21.6%, are genuine strengths this quarter. A weakness worth taking seriously, given it comes from a reasonably careful financial-data analysis rather than casual commentary, is that reliance on other income alongside, in some recent periods, negative core financing margins raises real earnings-quality questions; a further weakness is that combined foreign and domestic institutional ownership is only about 10.8%, suggesting comparatively lower institutional conviction than most peers in this document. One important, unresolved data point: gross NPA is reported as 2.47% by one source and 6.60% by another for the same period, and this discrepancy could not be resolved in this research pass — it should be checked against the bank's own primary filing before being relied upon. A further threat, assessed with moderate confidence, is simply the bank's small scale relative to its small-finance-bank peers, limiting economies of scale.

Suryoday Small Finance Bank is exposed to reputational and operational risk tied to that unresolved GNPA discrepancy, and to ordinary credit risk; net interest margin was 4.21%, up from 4.06% a year earlier, and net NPA fell in a range of 1.14% to 1.27% depending on the source. CRISIL and ICRA ratings are both on file — a CRISIL update dated November 2025 and an ICRA update dated March 2025 — but neither's specific rating value could be extracted this session. Growth is expected to come from affordable-housing and MSME diversification; margin from the recovery trend from 4.06% to 4.21%; return on equity from sustaining the current quarter's profitability rebound; the key risk driver is the earnings-quality question around other income alongside the unresolved GNPA discrepancy; and the valuation-relevant fact is whether asset-quality clarity is achieved and institutional ownership builds over time. Suryoday Small Finance Bank is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a small-finance-bank license.

JANA SMALL FINANCE BANK LIMITED (ticker JSFB, NSE; 544118, BSE)

Jana Small Finance Bank, headquartered in Bengaluru, Karnataka, originated as Janalakshmi Financial Services, founded on 24 July 2006 and once India's largest microfinance institution, converting to a small finance bank and commencing banking operations on 28 March 2018. Ajay Chamanlal Kanwal has been MD & CEO since August 2017, and Chitra Talwar is non-executive independent part-time chairperson. The bank's promoter, Jana Holdings Limited, held 16.94% as of June 2026, down from 22.54% in March 2024 — a meaningful, ongoing dilution; other shareholders include Volrado Venture Partners Fund-III at 2.78%, ICICI Prudential Life Insurance at 1.54%, Ananta Capital Ventures Fund 1 at 1.46%, and Singularity Growth Opportunities Fund II at 1.34%. The bank has historically been backed by major private-equity investors TPG Capital and HarbourVest Partners, a long-standing capital relationship (source: Wikipedia).

The bank runs a diversified model with a digital-banking push branded "DigiGen," having moved beyond its pure microfinance origins into secured retail and MSME banking. Capital adequacy improved to 20.18% as of Q1 FY27, up from 19.38% in the prior quarter. A detailed segment-level breakdown of the loan book was not found this session; a net-interest-income figure of ₹15,145 crore extracted from one source appears inconsistent with the bank's actual scale and is not reported here as fact, though the underlying growth rate cited alongside it, 22.07% year-on-year, is retained as directionally informative.

Profit after tax of ₹155.23 crore, up 52.29% year-on-year, and full-year FY26 profit of ₹326 crore, represent a genuine, high-confidence turnaround for a bank with a historically volatile earnings record — Jana suffered a large loss, around ₹2,504 crore, during the 2016 demonetization period, and has only more recently returned to consistent quarterly profitability. The clearest weakness is that FY26 return on equity was only 8%, well below several peers in this document — Ujjivan Small Finance Bank's 18.2%, for instance — and, despite repeated profits, the bank pays no dividend at all, which may reflect a continued capital-conservation priority. The opportunity, assessed with only moderate confidence since it rests on the existence of the DigiGen initiative rather than measured cost savings, is that the digital-banking platform could support more cost-efficient scaling; the threat is the continued dilution of the promoter's holding, from 22.54% to 16.94% between March 2024 and June 2026, a governance and ownership-stability point worth watching.

Jana Small Finance Bank is exposed to credit risk and to reputational risk tied to its historically volatile earnings record; it depends on its long-standing private-equity investor base, TPG Capital and HarbourVest Partners in particular. Other Q1 FY27 metrics: gross NPA 2.39% (improved from 2.46% in the prior quarter), net NPA 0.91% (from 0.92%), earnings per share ₹14.74. No current, dated credit rating from CRISIL, CARE, ICRA or Fitch could be independently confirmed this session, though a financial-data aggregator references "recent ratings updates from CARE, Fitch, and ICRA (latest July 2026)" without giving retrievable values. Growth is expected to come from digital-banking-led customer acquisition and secured-MSME expansion; margin depends on the balance between legacy microfinance yields and a rising secured mix, detail on which was not found; return on equity needs to improve materially from its current 8% level; the key risk driver is the continued promoter-holding dilution alongside the bank's historically volatile earnings track record; and the valuation-relevant fact is whether the bank's recent run of quarterly profits proves durable. Jana Small Finance Bank is regulated by the RBI and SEBI and holds a small-finance-bank license.

A handful of figures across this document were internally inconsistent across the sources checked and are called out rather than resolved, because getting them wrong would be worse than leaving them open: Bank of India's standalone (₹3,068 crore) versus a separately reported consolidated (₹1,747.39 crore) Q1 FY27 net profit; Union Bank of India's Q1 FY27 advances and deposit-growth figures, which rest on a single social-media-sourced citation rather than the bank's own investor presentation; IDBI Bank's secondary-sourced Q1 FY27 profit figure (₹2,115 crore), which does not reconcile with its FY24-25 full-year profit (₹7,515 crore); Jammu & Kashmir Bank's Q1 FY27 net-interest-income figure, which looks too large for the bank's balance-sheet scale; Jana Small Finance Bank's extracted net-interest-income figure, similarly inconsistent with its scale; Suryoday Small Finance Bank's gross-NPA figure, reported as both 2.47% and 6.60% by different sources for the same period; and Utkarsh Small Finance Bank's credit-rating outlook, where ICRA (Negative) and CARE (an A- Stable assignment) appear to diverge.

Fields most commonly marked "not found" across the document include exact current CASA ratios for several banks, return on assets/equity and cost-to-income for a number of banks where only headline profit growth was disclosed, current-dated credit ratings for roughly a dozen of the 39 banks, precise branch and ATM counts where the most recent verifiable figure predates 2025, non-executive chairman names for several public-sector banks (where turnover is frequent and disclosure often lags), and ISIN numbers, which were not systematically verified for any of the 39 companies.

On scope: this document covers all Public Sector, Private Sector and Small Finance Bank tickers identified in the underlying research — 39 companies. Fino Payments Bank Limited (ticker FINOPB), a listed Payments Bank, was not included, since the research was scoped to those three segments only; no Foreign Bank has a standalone Indian listing at all, so that segment is structurally empty rather than omitted. Most figures throughout refer to the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (Q1 FY27), the most recent common reporting period available when this was compiled on 15 September 2026.
